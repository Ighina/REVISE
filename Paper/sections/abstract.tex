\begin{abstract}
When retrieved evidence becomes sufficient to answer a question, does a language model undergo an internal state transition that predicts whether it will use that evidence, before it generates a token? We study this with paired forward passes over semantically defined \emph{evidence trajectories} for multi-hop questions: for each question we present nested contexts that add distractors, individual reasoning hops, the final decisive hop, redundant support, falsified evidence, or the answer string in an irrelevant passage, and we read the residual stream at the answer-start position. Linear probes on the paired activation update $\Delta h = h(C_k)-h(C_{k-1})$ recover \emph{sufficiency} (the increment that completes the evidence) at $0.95$ AUROC on document-, answer-entity- and relation-disjoint splits, transfer without retraining to a conversational protocol, to a second dataset and to deeper questions, and replicate across three model families. A fine-tuned text classifier, however, recovers sufficiency almost as well. A second, orthogonal direction predicts \emph{uptake}, whether decisive evidence will actually be integrated rather than ignored, at $0.78$ AUROC; here text models stay near chance ($0.56$), at full budget $0.63$, so uptake is a genuinely internal signal. Causal tests then separate readout from mechanism. The discriminative probe directions are inert under steering and projection-out in every model, whereas patching the full anchor residual is causally sufficient and its effect lies entirely in the orthogonal complement of the probe direction, in a high-rank subspace that on some models is dominated by the answer's unembedding direction. Only the mean-difference sufficiency direction is causally active, and it behaves as an abstention gate: projecting it out silences answering, and steering along it raises answering on distractors and false evidence as much as on sufficient evidence. Every finding replicates on HotpotQA, on RAGBench's naturally retrieved documents across five domains including single-hop questions, and on a synthetic benchmark with paraphrase, partial and contradictory evidence, although directions learned on Wikipedia transfer only partially to other domains. High-AUROC, transferable probes thus find the label, not the lever, and the one lever we find controls whether the model answers, not whether it has accommodated the evidence.
\end{abstract}
